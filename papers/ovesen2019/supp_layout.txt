                                SUPPLEMENTARY NOTE 2. EXCITONIC WAVEFUNCTIONS

The excitonic wavefunctions are obtained through solving the Wannier equations, cf. Supplementary Figure 2. A
general trend that can be observed is that a lower binding energy leads to sharper excitonic wavefunctions in the
momentum space corresponding to more delocalized excitonic states in the real space. A consequence of this is that
interactions involving interlayer excitons will favor scattering events with lower momentum transfers.

         SUPPLEMENTARY NOTE 3. TEMPERATURE DEPENDENCE OF EXCITON DYNAMICS

While in the main text, intra- and interlayer exciton dynamics and photoluminescence has been shown at 77 K, here
we present the momentum integrated dynamics calculated at 4 K and 300 K. Supplementary Figure 3 shows how the
coherence lifetime drastically increases at very low temperatures, which can be seen by comparing the red dashed
lines in Supplementary Figure 3(a) and Supplementary Figure 3(b). This behavior can be traced back to the reduced
efficiency of exciton-phonon scattering, which also explains the less pronounced polarization-to-population transfer,
i.e. the formation of incoherent excitons (red solid lines).

   This has a direct impact on the photoluminescence, which is presented in Supplementary Figure 4. At 4 K (Sup-
plementary Figure 4(a - c)) exciton-phonon scattering becomes significantly less efficient relative to the interlayer
tunneling. As a result, there is a non-negligible delay until interlayer excitons scatter into the light cone and become
visible in PL. As a result, the IeX emission does not saturate until it surpasses the IaX resonance at 35 ps (blue
line in Supplementary Figure 4(b)). Instead it slowly increases until it reaches equilibrium at approximately 60 ps
(purple line in Supplementary Figure 4(b)). This increase can be seen in more detail in Supplementary Figures 4(a,
c). At 300 K (Supplementary Figures 4(d - f)) the IeX resonance can barely be seen due to the broadening of the
intralayer exciton transition. This can be well seen in Supplementary Figure 4(f). In contrast to 4 K and 77 K, the
total emission at the IeX energy including off-resonant IaX emission (dashed blue line) exceeds the emission from IeX
alone (solid blue line).

                                         SUPPLEMENTARY NOTE 4. DFT CALCULATIONS

The structure consisting of a MoSe2 and a WSe2 layer in a 2H configuration was relaxed in the software VASP [1]
with both PBE and vdW-DF-cx until the maximal force was 5 meV/Å. The plane wave cutoff energy was 300 eV and
the Brillouin zone was sampled with a 15 × 15 × 5 k-point mesh. The PBE bilayer (with in-plane lattice constant
3.32 Å) was then isolated and a vacuum of 10 Å was applied to the bilayer. One monolayer was then shifted such
                                                                                                                                                         5

that the Mo-W distance matched the value obtained from the relaxation with the vdW-DF-cx functional (6.45 Å).
The bilayer system was then split up into two configurations consisting of a single layer. The wavefunctions of the
separate layers were then computed on a real space grid with GPAW [2] using norm conserving potentials and a grid
spacing of 0.2 Å-1. The k-point mesh for the self-consistent calculation of the electron density was 33 × 33 × 1. The
wavefunctions were then computed on a radial grid centered at K that extended 0.4 Å-1. The radial grid consisted
of 25 radial points (including K) and 40 angular points. The value of the overlap integral (for a smaller k-point set)
was checked with a reduced grid spacing of 0.1 Å-1.
